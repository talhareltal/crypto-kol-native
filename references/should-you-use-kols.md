# Should you use KOLs at all?

Start with the goal, not the KOL list.

## By goal

| Goal | KOLs? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Token launch awareness with retail | Often yes | Retail traders live on X and Telegram. Speed matters. |
| Users for a live consumer product | Maybe | Works if the KOL actually uses it and shows it. Test small first. |
| Developer adoption | Rarely | Developers trust docs, demos and other developers. Sponsor builders, not KOLs. |
| Institutional or investor credibility | No | Funds and partners read KOL promos as a warning sign. Earned media and data work better. |
| Long-term brand | Rarely | Paid posts disappear in a day. Brand comes from repetition and substance. |
| Hiring | No | Use your own team's voices. |
| Fixing a reputation problem | No | Paid defenders make it worse. |

## When KOLs make sense

- The product is live and someone can try it in five minutes.
- You can track what happens after the click (wallet, signup, deposit).
- The KOL's audience is the user you want, in the market you serve.
- You have budget to test small before going big.

## When to skip

- Nothing is live yet. You're paying for attention you can't convert.
- Your real goal is credibility with investors or partners.
- The plan only works if the token price goes up.
- You can't measure past impressions.
- The KOL agency's pitch is a follower count and a price.

## The cheaper alternatives

- Your founder posting every day with real substance
- Getting your actual users to post (and sharing them)
- One earned media story
- Sponsoring a niche newsletter or podcast your users already read
- Partnering with a project that shares your users

Compare the KOL quote against these before you pay.
