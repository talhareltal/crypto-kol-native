# Vetting a KOL

What to check before you pay anyone.

## Audience

- **Who follows them?** Traders, builders, normies, other KOLs? Read 30 random followers.
- **Where are they?** If you can't serve a market (regulation, language), followers there are worth zero to you.
- **Growth pattern.** Steady growth is normal. Sudden jumps of tens of thousands with no viral post behind it suggest bought followers.

## Engagement

- **Compare to peers,** not to a fixed number. A KOL with 10x the followers of a peer and the same likes is a warning.
- **Read the replies.** Real questions, arguments and jokes are good. Rows of "LFG", "bullish", rocket emoji from accounts with no avatar and default names are not.
- **Views vs likes.** Very high views with almost no replies often means views were boosted.
- **Who replies.** The same 20 accounts on every post can mean an engagement group.

## Track record

- **What did they promote before?** Scroll back three to six months.
- **How did those projects do after?** Still alive? Users still there? Token still trading?
- **Deleted posts.** Promos that vanished after a project went bad are a signal.
- **Ad density.** If more than a few posts a week are paid, the audience has learned to scroll past.
- **Category fit.** A DeFi KOL promoting a gaming project rarely moves gaming users.

## Behaviour

- **Disclosure history.** Do they mark paid posts? If they don't for others, they won't for you, and the risk is yours.
- **Price talk.** KOLs who post price targets for sponsored tokens bring legal and reputational risk.
- **Controversies.** Search their handle with "scam", "rug", "paid", "exposed". Read what you find, don't just count it.
- **How they talk to their audience.** Do they answer questions? Admit mistakes?

## Shortlist format

Return a table: KOL, audience fit, engagement quality, track record, red flags, verdict (Worth it / Test small / Pass).

## Never

- Call a named KOL fake or a scammer. Describe what you see.
- Treat follower count as the deciding factor.
